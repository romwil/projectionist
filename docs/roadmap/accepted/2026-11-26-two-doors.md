# Drop: two doors — people come through the front door, apps through the app door

**From:** Will (via design chat) · **Date:** 2026-11-26 · **Type:** architecture + security · **Supersedes:** the "remove Cloudflare Access" end state of the public-front-door plan (Access stays, permanently, for this household)

## Why

The owner keeps Cloudflare Access in front of everything people use. Native apps can't complete an interactive Access login in their own network stack, and AVPlayer/AirPlay can't send extra headers. Rather than weakening Access, split the surfaces: **interactive, human surfaces** behind whatever gate the household uses, and a **minimal, credential-only API surface** for apps. The same split is what makes Projectionist safe for other homelabbers behind VPNs, reverse proxies with forward-auth, or plain port forwards.

## Decisions (accepted)

### 1. Two listeners

- **Front door, `:8788` (unchanged):** the web app, sign-in (all methods), invites, onboarding, admin, setup, and everything else today.
- **App door, new `:8789`** (configurable; off unless enabled, or on by default with clear docs; decide and record):
  - serves **only** client API routes that require a **device credential** (bearer), plus the **engine and library media token URLs**, plus the minimal Apple TV pairing routes (`pair/issue`, `pair/rotate`, `pair/pending/{id}`);
  - **no** HTML, no SPA, no static assets, no sign-in routes, no invites, no access requests, no setup, no admin, no MCP, no webhooks, no OpenAPI;
  - **cookies are ignored** (never accepted as auth) and no cookies are set;
  - route membership comes from the existing client-route tags (NAT-003). A test asserts every route on the app door is tagged and bearer-only, and that every untagged route 404s there.
  - CORS: none (native clients only).
- Shared app process, so one container. The listener split is enforced by middleware on the second port's app instance.

### 2. How apps get credentials without the app door offering sign-in

- iPhone, iPad and Mac sign in in `ASWebAuthenticationSession` against the **front door** (which may sit behind Access or a forward-auth proxy; the sheet is a browser, so that works), then receive the device credential through the existing native flow.
- Apple TV shows a pair code, approved on the front door by a signed-in member; the TV then uses the app door only.
- The device credential is scoped to the client API, revocable in Settings → Devices, shown with its last use; rotation as today.
- **Server addresses** published to signed-in clients: `home` (LAN), `away` (front door, for sign-in), and **`app_away`** (the app door's public URL). Apps call the API on whichever of home or app_away answers, and open sign-in on home or away.

### 3. Safe when exposed carelessly (the community baseline)

- All public-front-door gates remain required (they're now the baseline for any exposed instance, and defense in depth here).
- **HTTPS required off-LAN:** a cookie sign-in from a non-LAN client over plain HTTP is refused with a link to the remote-access guide. The app door refuses non-HTTPS from non-LAN clients too.
- **Exposure card** in Admin → Health ("How exposed am I?"), green or red with fix links:
  - external traffic seen, and whether it arrives over HTTPS;
  - login on, every owner with a second factor, MCP LAN-only;
  - no Docker socket, the library read-only;
  - the app door separate, the front door gated (Access, forward-auth or VPN detected when possible), the media-token TTL on.

### 4. Easy connection for households

- **Add a device by QR:** Settings → Devices → "Add a device" shows a QR or link carrying the server's home, away and app_away addresses, plus a **one-time, short-lived pairing token** (single use, ~10 min). The app scans it (iPhone and iPad camera, Mac paste) and completes sign-in on the front door with that context. No typing of server URLs.
- **LAN discovery:** advertise `_projectionist._tcp` via mDNS/Bonjour, with the server name and a fingerprint, no secrets. Document that it needs a network mode that can advertise (Unraid br0/macvlan or host). Apps list discovered servers on first run.
- **`docs/REMOTE-ACCESS.md`**, with tested, copy-paste configs:
  1. Tailscale/VPN (recommended): HTTPS via Tailscale's certificates; addresses to enter.
  2. Cloudflare Tunnel + Access: front-door hostname behind Access; app-door hostname with no Access plus edge rules (WAF, rate limit, path allow-list `/api/…` client routes and media paths).
  3. Reverse proxy (Nginx Proxy Manager, SWAG, Traefik, Caddy), with an Authelia or Authentik forward-auth example: protect the front door, pass the app door through.
  4. Port forwarding: supported, discouraged; what the exposure card will say.

### 5. This household's plan changes

- **Cloudflare Access stays on `projectionist.automat.vip` permanently.** Remove "Access removal" from the change plan. Members without WARP use **Access email one-time codes** (or the Workspace) in browsers and in the apps' sign-in sheet.
- Add **`app.projectionist.automat.vip` → `:8789`** to the change plan, with no Access, plus the edge rules above, after the app door ships and passes the pentest.
- The WARP private route becomes optional (decide later).
- Pentest v2 scope adds the app door: unauthenticated surface = 401/404 only; path allow-list enforced at the edge and in the app; cookie auth refused; media tokens; pairing routes.

## Done when

- The app door serves only tagged bearer routes, media tokens and pairing (tests).
- An iPhone signs in through an Access-gated front door in the sheet and then streams over the app door with AirPlay and PiP working.
- Apple TV pairs and plays over the app door.
- QR add-a-device works; Bonjour discovery works on br0.
- The exposure card is accurate in lab scenarios (LAN-only, tunnel + Access, raw port forward over HTTP).
- The remote-access guide configs are tested in a lab.
