## 3. Foundations nobody challenged hard enough

The platform map listed 26 sacred cows. Departments mostly protected them. Three deserved a harder push:

1. **Live as a core surface (SC-13).** Live is the newest and largest surface (its own CSS, guide, player, scheduler, and a Tunarr dependency). Every department that looked at it found defects: hidden nav, guide semantics, focus rings, an architectural boundary, and a scope warning. Nobody asked the blunt question: **should Live be a lab feature until the core curator is trustworthy?** This is now Big Decision BD-1, with "Freeze" as the recommended answer.
2. **Single-owner, no-login as the default first run.** It is convenient and it is the reason SEC-010 exists. Security raised it, and nobody else weighed the growth cost against the risk. This is now BD-2.
3. **Chat as the front door.** Every department accepted it. Product's own top finding (PRD-001) is that the "why" text is wrong, and UX measured one prompt. The review never tested whether Explore, not chat, is where members actually start. This is an open question (Q2 below), not a decision. There is no data to decide it.

## 5. A bolder alternative the review did not propose

**One "trust release" before any new feature.** It would bundle the following, and nothing else:

- **E1:** a single authorization policy shared by REST, agent tools, confirmation tokens, and the Youth toggle.
- **E2:** a Privacy page that matches the code.
- **E4:** household evidence never phrased as personal.
- **E5:** scheduled backups plus one restore drill.

Each department proposed its slice of this. Nobody proposed doing only this, and the combined effort is roughly one release's worth. The executive summary adopts it as the **Now** horizon.

## 6. Questions the review cannot answer

1. **Q1:** How many real households run unauthenticated single-owner mode on a LAN with untrusted devices? (Sizes BD-2.)

3. **Q3:** What share of plays happen in native Plex versus in-browser Play? (Sizes GRO-010 and the headline metric.)
