## 0. Verdict on the review itself

The board did careful, well-cited work. Security, Privacy, Architecture, and Performance are the strongest documents, and their headline findings (SEC-001, SEC-002, PRIV-001 with ARCH-009, PERF-008, ARCH-007) are backed by code and, where possible, by live measurement.

The weakness is **context, not diligence**. Every department surveyed one degraded box:

- The secrets key could not decrypt the TMDB key (OPS-010, OPS-011).
- No LLM was configured.
- Live was Off.
- The accounts were synthetic `qa-*` users who had never rated anything.
- The database had been seeded by earlier pentest and adversarial QA runs.

Several departments then described that box as "the product". In the most important cases (§1.1), a finding is real in the code but its **severity or framing** comes from the environment. The roadmap should not be approved until the environment-sensitive items are re-run once on a correctly configured install (Question 1, §7).

The board also stayed cautious on scope. About 310 findings across ten documents are mostly "fix it in place". Only Product proposes removing whole surfaces, and only as "park behind ready". For a one-maintainer project shipping about 1.7 releases per day (ARCH-007), the real constraint is capacity. Too few documents said so plainly.

## 2. Sacred cows the experts failed to challenge, or kept without good reason

1. **#16 "For you must never fill with cosine" and named kinship as "the moat"** (PRD-001, UX-002, GRO-005, VIS, OPS). Every department protects it, and most cite **one screenshot** (`qa_owner_explore_1440.png`, "adam McKay worked on both"). The board's own evidence shows what the rule costs:
   - Kinship coverage on opened titles is **17%** (OPS-001, OPS-012).
   - The youth account's For you was **empty**: "No named link to something you finished — not a cosine guess." (UX-044 evidence).
   - New members have no finished titles, so they get the same empty rail.

   The board's headline recommendation is to make Explore For you the default landing (PRD-006, UX §4.1, UX-031). That collides directly with this rule: the activation persona would land on an empty rail. Nobody connected the two. The cow may still be right, because honesty is a real differentiator. But it needs a declared fallback (Tonight, Continue Watching, or a clearly labelled "popular in your library" pick) before it becomes the front door.

2. **#10 Native Live without Tunarr.** It was kept or parked without being seen working. Live is about 27k lines and 13% of the code (ARCH open question 4). Prod backups are still dominated by 3.7 GB of Tunarr state (PERF-009), so the migration away from Tunarr is not finished on the maintainer's own host. A feature this large deserves an explicit keep-or-cut call, not "park behind ready".

3. **#9 Youth is a flag, enforced by Projectionist.** Every department accepted that Projectionist must build its own youth enforcement layer: a policy object (PRIV-008, ARCH-015), gates on about 40 call sites (SEC-018), re-gating history, and gates on newsletters. **No one asked whether Plex Managed Users' content restrictions should be the source of truth for the rating ceiling.** That would make Plex itself refuse playback, and Projectionist would only mirror the ceiling for recommendations. This matters because the "Plex-first" cow (#7) is already accepted.

4. **#17 The Monday pass.** OPS-001, UX-016, PRD-014, and OPS-030 all protect and expand it. The evidence that it works is the maintainer's own habit plus Help copy. Nothing shows other owners do a weekly 20-minute stewardship pass. A checklist with done states (OPS-030) is cheap. The rest of the Monday-pass investment (UX-036, OPS-021, OPS-012 shortlist) should wait for a signal that someone other than the maintainer uses it.

5. **#5 Zero telemetry.** GRO keeps no phone-home and rejects even an opt-in install ping (GRO open question 7). That is principled, but it means **no roadmap item in this review can be validated across installs**. The local event spine (GRO-007, GRO-014, GRO-025) shows one owner their own house of two to six people. It does not tell the maintainer whether UX-009 is losing installers, which UX's own Risks section flags ("the project won't see it happen"). The board kept the cow without pricing what it costs to be blind.

6. **#8 "Multi-user is opt-in" is already dead in code, and the board did not notice.** Security shows the setup wizard forces `multi_user = True` (`setup_mode.py:276`). Privacy ("Keep — correct default") and UX ("Keep… no change") ruled on the cow as if it were still live. ARCH-008's worst case, falling back to single-owner on a parse error, matters more precisely because single-owner is no longer a mode users choose.

7. **#15 MCP dual keys.** Security, Privacy, and Architecture all keep the design. PRIV-014 shows the full key exposes every member's watchlist pins and watch patterns to whatever client holds it, often a cloud desktop app. No one asked whether full-mode MCP belongs **on by default** in a household product, rather than as an owner opt-in that comes with a member notice.

8. **#13 Release per PR.** ARCH-007 and PERF-010 challenge the gate, not the cadence. Shipping 155 version bumps in 90 days to Unraid CA users is itself a support load and a QA cost. ARCH-007 recommends batching only "while gates are red". The bolder ruling is a weekly train from green `main` as the default (§4).

## 3. Where the review anchored on the existing implementation

- **Onboarding** (UX-009, UX-013, UX-036). The board redesigns two wizards into one wizard. It does not ask whether a CA/Docker product should be **configured by its template env vars first**, with the UI showing what was detected. Wizards would then exist only for what env can't carry.
- **Admin IA** (UX §4.2). The plan regroups all 18 destinations into new groups. No department proposes **deleting** destinations. Holidays, Lobby, Youth (as a separate page), Advanced, and Mail versus Newsletters could each fold away.
- **Authorization** (SEC-002, ARCH-015, ARCH-022). The proposed kernel is designed around today's three entry points (REST, agent, MCP). The cheaper question nobody asked first: **should the agent hold destructive tools at all?** If `mark_bad_media`, `approve_seerr_request`, and `remove_from_arr` become "propose only, the UI acts", the most serious privilege escalation closes in days, without waiting for an L-sized kernel. SEC-002's recommendation points this way. The roadmap should make it step one.
- **Continue Watching and "you watched" leaks** (SEC-010, PRIV-006, UX-044). Every recommendation filters or rewords the household-wide on-deck. What should exist is **per-member Plex identity**, using Plex Home user tokens, so on-deck and history are per person at the source. Filtering a household feed treats the symptom.
- **Analytics** (GRO-007, GRO-014, GRO-025). The design extends the existing telemetry tables into a spine plus an owner Pulse page. It starts from "what can we record" rather than "what decision does the maintainer need to make". For example: is UX-009 losing installs? Do configured households use chat or Explore?
- **Design system** (VIS-003, VIS-006, VIS-020, VIS-021). The plan is incremental token surgery on 20,598 lines of global CSS. That is reasonable. Still, no "if we built it today" section in Visual or Architecture asked whether a small set of component primitives with co-located styles should replace the layered sheets for new work. The token pass is anchored on the sheets as they are.
- **"If we built it today" sections** in ARCH, PERF, and SEC mostly describe today's app with its seams fixed. That is an honest result for the stack. But it means **no department zero-based the product surface**, meaning which surfaces should exist at all. Product came closest, with the cut list in PRD-013.

## 4. The bolder option the board was too cautious to recommend

**Freeze and cut for one quarter.**

1. **Stop shipping new surfaces.** Ship a weekly release train only from green `main` (ARCH-007 and PERF-010 as policy, not exception).
2. **Remove, rather than park, the surfaces nobody evaluated working:** Live Channels, Lobby Theater, Holidays, Whisper (folded into Inbox), and My Journey. Each can come back behind its own evidence gate.
3. **Spend the reclaimed capacity on the five things every department agrees on:**
   - Agent and stream authorization: SEC-001, and SEC-002 done as propose-only.
   - Youth parity on every outbound channel: GRO-008, PRIV-007, UX-044.
   - Real deletion: PRIV-001 and ARCH-009.
   - A library-first home that works without an LLM and has a declared For you fallback: UX-010, UX-031, and §2.1 above.
   - Compression plus fixing the blocked event loop: PERF-007 and PERF-008.

**Why it's bolder than the board:** Product says "park behind ready", which keeps all the code, tests, and churn. Architecture says to freeze growth of god files, not features. Nobody recommended deleting code. Live alone is about 13% of the codebase and a large share of churn.

**Risks:**

- **Live is the most differentiated feature** against Jellyseerr, Diskovarr, and Shortlist (platform map §5), and the maintainer's own household likely uses it. Cutting it may cost the one reason some CA installers chose Projectionist. Without telemetry (§2.5), nobody can know.
- **Removal is not cheaply reversible.** Code and data paths rot outside `main`, and `live_lineup.json` and the lineup state need a migration-safe exit (ARCH-014).
- **Existing CA users lose a feature in a minor release.** That needs a deprecation release with clear notes, not a silent removal.
- **Mitigation:** cut behind a feature flag for one release, publish the reason in CHANGELOG Highlights, and keep the branch. Re-admit Live only after it is reviewed On.

A second bold option, smaller and lower risk: **delegate the youth rating ceiling and per-person watch identity to Plex** (Managed Users plus Home user tokens, §2.3 and §3). Risk: local-password and OIDC members with no Plex user have no ceiling source, so a fallback is still needed. It also deepens Plex lock-in, though sacred cow #7 already accepts that.

## 7. Three questions leadership should ask before approving the roadmap

1. **"Which of these findings still reproduce on a correctly configured install — TMDB key decrypting, an LLM attached, and Live On?"** Several Highs and both chat-related "Change" rulings were formed on a box with broken secrets, no LLM, Live Off, synthetic users, and pentest-seeded data (§1.1). One day of Path B QA on a healthy config would confirm or downgrade VIS-027, PRD-007, PRD-011, OPS-010, OPS-014, PRD-005, and the chat-home decision before any of them shape the plan.

2. **"Who is this roadmap for: the maintainer's own Automat household, or the unknown CA installers behind about 19k pulls and 1 GitHub star — and how will we know whether it worked, given that nothing leaves the box?"** The answer decides whether the installer path (UX-009, UX-013), the public-hostname bytes (PERF-007, PERF-011), Live, the Monday pass, and the analytics spine are Now or never. It also decides whether to revisit the opt-in install ping that GRO declined.

3. **"With one maintainer, what fits in the next quarter, and what are we willing to stop or remove to make room?"** That means a release train from green `main` instead of per-PR releases, and an explicit keep-or-cut decision on Live, Lobby, Holidays, Whisper, and My Journey. Against roughly 310 findings, a roadmap that cuts nothing is not a plan. The non-negotiable core (SEC-001, SEC-002, the youth channel gaps, PRIV-001/ARCH-009, UX-010, PERF-007/008) should be funded first and in full.
